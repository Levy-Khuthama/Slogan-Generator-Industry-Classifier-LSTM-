# Slogan-Generator-Industry-Classifier-LSTM-
Two LSTM neural networks built with TensorFlow/Keras on a dataset of company slogans:

Slogan generator – given an industry (e.g. "internet"), generates a slogan one word at a time.
Industry classifier – given a slogan, predicts which industry the company belongs to.

The two models are then chained together: generate a slogan for an industry, then ask the classifier whether it recognises the industry.

This project was completed as the capstone for the Artificial Intelligence online bootcamp (Stellenbosch University in partnership with HyperionDev).

Approach

Preprocessing

Cleaned the data (dropped rows with missing slogans/industries) and kept the output (slogan) and industry columns.
Used spaCy to lowercase and tokenise each slogan and strip punctuation.
For the generator, prefixed each slogan with its industry name so the model learns the link between industry and wording.
Converted text to integer sequences with the Keras Tokenizer and padded to equal length.
For the classifier, removed industries with fewer than two slogans so a stratified 80/20 train/test split was possible.

Generator

Trained on progressively longer partial slogans to predict the next word.
Architecture: Embedding(100) → LSTM(150, return_sequences=True) → LSTM(100) → Dense(softmax) over the vocabulary.
Categorical cross-entropy loss, Adam optimiser, 50 epochs, batch size 128.
Text is generated by repeatedly picking the most probable next word (greedy decoding).

Classifier

Trained on complete padded slogans, labelled by one-hot encoded industry (136 classes).
Same layer layout as the generator, with a softmax output of one unit per industry.
Categorical cross-entropy loss, Adam optimiser, 50 epochs, batch size 128.
Results
Model	Result
Classifier – training accuracy	~89%
Classifier – test accuracy	~18% (test loss 5.94 vs 0.55 on training)
Generator – training accuracy	~54%

For context, random guessing across 136 industries would be about 0.7%, and always predicting the most common industry would be about 8.5%.

Key takeaways
The classifier overfits. The gap between training and test accuracy shows it memorises the training slogans rather than generalising. Causes: a small dataset (about 4,300 training slogans) spread over many industries, many with very few examples, and no regularisation.
The generator drifts. Slogans start with the right industry words, then become repetitive or unrelated because of greedy word selection.
The combined pipeline is unreliable. For example, a slogan generated for "internet" was classified as a different industry.
Ideas for improvement
Add dropout and early stopping to reduce overfitting.
Use more data, or group the 136 industries into broader categories.
Sample with temperature (instead of always taking the top word) when generating slogans.
Try pretrained word embeddings.
